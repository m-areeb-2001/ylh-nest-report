Why I am asking

The yellow-legged hornet (Vespa velutina) is an invasive species first found in Georgia in 2023. It preys on honey bees and other pollinators. Its nests hang high in trees and are hard to spot from the ground, which is exactly the problem my thesis works on.

1. You report a nest

Address or map pin, and a photo if it is safe to take one. Name and contact details are optional but let me follow up.

2. I get in touch first

If the site fits the study I will contact you before anything happens. No one visits or flies without your permission.

3. A short drone survey

A few minutes of photographs from the air (normal and thermal). The images train and test the detector. Nothing is sprayed and the nest is not touched.

Please also report every sighting to the Georgia Department of Agriculture, which handles removal: agr.georgia.gov/yellow-legged-hornet. Not sure it is a yellow-legged hornet? Send the photo anyway.

What to look for

Quick identification, based on the Georgia Department of Agriculture description.

The hornet

Dark body, legs partly or mostly yellow, orange-yellow face. Smaller than the European hornet, which is larger with more yellow and brown on the body.

The nest

Egg-shaped paper nest with a shell-like outside. Softball to watermelon size in early summer, larger than a basketball by late summer and fall, usually high in a tree.

Look-alikes

Bald-faced hornets build grey nests too but are black and white. Paper wasps build small open combs under eaves. If in doubt, report it and we will check.
